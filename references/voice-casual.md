# Casual Voice Guide (for series posts and articles)

Use this with `references/voice.md`. Goal: sound like Mike talking to one person, not presenting to a room.

Built from Mike's own sent mail (2025-2026). Names removed. Phase 1: a drafter loads this by hand until Mike confirms it cuts edit passes.

## The test

Read the draft out loud. If Mike would not say it across a table, rewrite it.

## Rules

- Talk to one person. Use "you", "I", "we".
- Contractions always: I'll, we're, don't, it's.
- One or two sentences per paragraph. One idea each.
- Plain words. If a short word works, use it.
- Get to the point first. No windup, no "in today's landscape".
- Say what happened, then what it meant. Skip the framing.
- Ask direct questions. Numbered if there are several.
- Warm, not polished. A small rough edge is fine. Don't sand it into corporate.
- No em dashes. Bullets over paragraphs for lists.
- No headers that sound like a slide ("Key Takeaways", "The Framework").

## Real samples (from Mike's sent mail)

> Attached is my updated resume. Note that I have taken some time off for personal reasons and am now back looking for a new opportunity.

Plain. States the situation, no spin.

> Thank you for the detailed feedback. This is incredibly helpful insight.

Warm, short, means it.

> Either the church or a library would work for me. Just tell me when and where.

Easy and direct. Makes the other person's life simple.

> Sorry, I do not know anyone.

Short is fine.

> It works for me.

Same.

> Hope you and your family are well.
>
> I'd love to get your feedback on our business plan. Specifically, I'd value your thoughts on:
> • Competitive positioning: How credible is our differentiation against the big boards on cost?
> • Revenue timing: Is month 2 for payments aggressive, or realistic given where the MVP is?

Specific, numbered questions. No vague asks.

## Say it like this, not like that

| Stilted | Casual |
|---|---|
| The process seamlessly integrates multiple verification stages. | We check it three ways before it moves on. |
| It is important to note that the tests passed. | The tests passed. |
| This approach leverages role-based accountability. | Each role owns one thing. |
| Furthermore, the gate enforces compliance. | And the gate blocks anything that skips a step. |
| A notable trend emerged across the sprint. | Same mistake kept showing up. |

## Ban list (these read as stilted)

seamlessly, intricate, multifaceted, robust, leverage (as a verb), delve, burgeoning, delineated, diligently, notable, "it is important to note", "in conclusion", "furthermore", "moreover", "in today's", "game-changer", "unlock", "dive in", "journey" (unless Mike says it).

## How to check a draft

1. Read it aloud. Mark every line Mike would not say.
2. Scan for the ban list and em dashes.
3. Count paragraphs over three sentences. Split them.
4. Does it open with the point? If not, cut the first paragraph and see if it still works.
